# Getting The Most Out Of Notes In NotebookLM

## Creating Notes

Currently, NotebookLM has two main categories of notes: Written Notes and Saved Responses. You can create a Written Note by clicking the "New Note" icon in the upper right corner of your notebook. Written Notes can have basic formatting, like bold and italics; you can paste text into a note if you want as well. Saved Responses can take two forms: quotes from the AI and quotes from your sources.

Anytime you are chatting with the NotebookLM AI and something interesting comes up during the conversation that you want to capture, you can hit the pin icon in the upper right corner of the chat response. That will automatically pin a new Saved Response note to the noteboard.

Saved responses are not editable, which lets you easily keep track of the provenance of your notes. (In many workflows, it's good to know what *you* wrote versus what the AI wrote.) If you want to edit the AI response or integrate it into your own writing, you can always click on the copy icon at the bottom of the response, and paste the text into a Written Note that you've created.

Saving interesting responses is an essential part of the NotebookLM design: it enables you to have an open-ended exploratory conversation with the AI, grounded in the sources that you've defined, while capturing all the important parts of the conversation as you go. It's a fantastic tool for brainstorming, particularly because NotebookLM also gives you powerful tools to organize the responses you save. You can quickly pin anything that strikes your fancy, and then revisit it all later to turn those disorganized fragments into something more structured.

Notes are also an important component of *reading* inside of NotebookLM. If you click on a source icon, the original document opens up and you can read it in its entirety. NotebookLM automatically gives you a source guide at the top, with a quick summary and list of topics to help you get your bearings. You can also explore sources by following the suggested questions that NotebookLM automatically generates. But you can also capture interesting quotes from your sources as you read. Just select text in the source, and you'll see a set of "suggested actions" in the chatbox.

Suggested actions when text is selected in a source:
- **Add to Note**: Captures the quote as a new Saved Response note
- **Summarize to Note**: Creates a bullet-point summary of the selected text
- **Help Me Understand**: Translates complicated passages into layperson language with a glossary of key terms
- **Suggest Related Ideas**: Suggests connections to other facts or concepts in your sources

## Organizing Your Notes

Once you've pinned a collection of notes to the noteboard, you can select them and use the AI to transform them in a wide variety of ways: you can create an automatic study guide, for instance, or a thematic outline; or you can type your own instructions into the chatbox to generate formats (or ask questions) based on the selected notes.

### Limitations and Workaround

If the combined text in your selected notes is more than 5,000 words, you will not be able to query them using the chatbox, and some features like "create study guide" may not work.

**Workaround for large note collections:**
1. Select all your notes using the "Select All Notes" button
2. Choose the "Combine to one note" suggested action
3. Open that new note and select all the text inside of it
4. Click the "Add Source" button and choose "Copied Text"; paste your copied notes and give it a name like "March 2024 notes"

At that point, you can treat all your notes like a traditional source, using the chatbox to ask questions or get summaries or transform your notes into whatever format you want.

**Example queries after converting notes to a source:**
- *Create a briefing doc summarizing all my notes that discuss the Sears trial*
- *What was the name of the Italian director I wrote about a few weeks ago?*
- *Suggest an outline for a marketing plan based on my notes from the meeting with Bill Conway*

This workaround also lets you access notes across notebooks: combine notes from one notebook, copy them, switch to another notebook, and create a new source there.

## Creating a Backup of Your Notes

The "combine to note" feature also gives a quick way to create a backup of notes or export them to another format. Follow the combine steps above, then paste the combined notes into another app like Google Docs.
